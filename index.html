Learn Negotiation with Phoebe

Every negotiation has two layers: what each side says it wants, and why. Most deals that fail, or settle for less than they could, never get below the first layer. Six sessions on the craft underneath: interests, the walk-away point that tells you when to leave, the first number in the room and how much it really moves the result, and the trades that let both sides finish ahead. The claims are tested, not repeated. A bench of 4,000 real negotiations between strangers, counted in your browser, shows the first-offer effect holding up, a shuffle proving it is real, and the friendliest rule in negotiation, meeting halfway, capturing 64.7% of the value that was on the table.

The six sessions 🎯 · from what they say to a deal both sides sign

The running case is a constructed one: Kestrel Foods, an invented grocery chain, negotiating a twelve-month carton supply contract with Brightwell Packaging, an invented supplier. Every price and cost in it is made up for teaching. The evidence around it is real: books and papers are traced to their source, and the bench runs on a public dataset of human negotiations.

🧭 Session 1 · foundation

Positions and interests

What each side says it wants, and why it wants it. The library window, the four principles of principled negotiation, and what the "Harvard method" label leaves out. Build-along: map both sides of a supplier contract.

▶ Start here 🚪 Session 2 · core

Walk-away points and the deal zone

Your best alternative, the price it implies, and the zone where a deal can exist. A calculator for the carton contract shows the zone, its width, and an opening number already outside it.

the deal-zone calculator 🎯 Session 3 · core

The first number in the room

Anchoring as the laboratory measured it, read at source, and the honest disagreement about whether to go first. How to open, and how to stop an opening number from setting your deal.

anchors, read at source 🎛️ Session 4 · bench night

The anchor bench

4,000 real negotiations between strangers, counted in your browser. The first offer predicts the final split, a shuffle proves it is real, and splitting the difference captures 64.7% of the value on the table.

★ the bench 🔁 Session 5 · advanced

Trade across issues

The fixed-pie assumption, why it is so common, and the trade that breaks it. Price against payment terms, volume and delivery, and a package both sides prefer to meeting halfway.

logrolling 🤝 Session 6 · core

Saying no, closing, and the next deal

A no that keeps the relationship, a close both sides remember the same way, and what the famous hostage-negotiator advice does and does not have evidence for. Final scorecard.
